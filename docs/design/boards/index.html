A few practical questions

Before you begin.

Do I need coding experience?

The current program is described as beginner-friendly and uses no-code tools. Discuss the right approach for your idea before joining.

How much time should I allow?

The current website describes about 2–3 hours weekly, including a one-hour mentoring session. Confirm the arrangement before joining.

Does this guarantee admissions or revenue?

No. A project can demonstrate initiative, but admissions, grants, publicity, and commercial results are not guaranteed.

How do I learn about fees and availability?

Request a conversation to confirm current details. This independent demo does not take payments or reserve a place.

Native disclosure specimens can be opened with a pointer or keyboard. They are not connected to a backend.

Getting started / Original demo content

Start smaller.
Learn faster.

A useful first version begins with a narrower question, not a longer feature list.

Demo editorial author · Illustrative article specimen · Reading time calculated in the app

It is tempting to describe your idea through everything it could eventually become. A booking system. A community. A marketplace. A place for everyone.

But the first version has a simpler job: help you discover whether one particular thing is useful to one particular group of people.

Choose the question before the features.

Imagine helping neighbours share books. Before building profiles, recommendations, and delivery tracking, ask whether people nearby want to lend the books they already own.

A small experiment can teach you something that another week of polishing cannot. Keep the question clear enough that you can recognise an answer.

Try this: describe the first version in one sentence, then remove anything you do not need to test that sentence.
